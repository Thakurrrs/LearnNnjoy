# LearnNnjoy — Mountain Rescue Natural-Fit Reassessment

**Status:** Mountain Rescue scope approved; replacement-world brainstorm rejected
**Scope:** Grade 7 integer concepts
**Constraint:** Story fit is more important than world or chapter count
**Implementation authority:** None

---

## 1. Outcome

Keep **Mountain Rescue** only for the integer relationships that occur
naturally on its mountain:

- positive, negative, and zero positions;
- position relative to a reference point;
- comparing and ordering signed positions;
- addition as successive directed movement;
- subtraction as reversing or adding the opposite movement.

Do not force integer multiplication, division, and operation properties into
the mountain.

The current **Supply Lift Timeline** and **Network Route Rules** remain useful
curriculum checklists, but they are not approved story premises.

Integer multiplication, division, and operation properties are deliberately
left **unassigned**. Replay Realm and GlitchGrid were both rejected because
their invented controls made the discussion more complicated than the
mathematics. No replacement world is required before Mountain Rescue can move
forward.

---

## 2. Natural-fit test for Mountain Rescue

### Child fantasy or activity

Ride an aurora rescue sled with Nova, follow a lost signal, recover a glowing
energy pod, and bring it back to Pip’s mountain shelter.

### Natural world behaviour

- Mountain locations sit above or below Base Camp.
- A sled or pod moves upward and downward from its current position.
- Gusts accumulate into a new position.
- A winch can reverse a previous direction.

### Mathematical relationship inside the behaviour

- Base Camp acts as zero.
- Positions above and below it need opposite signs.
- Higher and lower positions create integer order.
- Consecutive gusts combine as signed addition.
- Reversing a move creates its additive inverse and supports subtraction.

### Remove-the-Mathematics test

If signed position and directed movement are removed:

- the child cannot describe where the pod is relative to Base Camp;
- the beacon order has no dependable rule;
- gust movements cannot be accumulated;
- the winch reversal cannot be represented consistently.

The relationship is therefore necessary to the rescue, not an inserted quiz.

### Mountain verdict

**Pass** for position, order, addition, and subtraction.

### Where Mountain stops fitting

- “Negative cycles” require a timeline explanation that is not obvious from a
  mountain rescue.
- Integer multiplication properties do not naturally matter to recovering the
  pod.
- Reordering delivery routes is not the same as a child wanting to explore
  multiplication structure.
- The sequel requires Nova to explain why the controls represent signs before
  the child can understand the story goal.

These are signs that curriculum mapping came before story selection.

## 4. Rejected concept record — Replay Realm

> **Do not develop.** This record is historical context, not a product plan.

### Short world explanation

Nova and the child enter a colourful animation studio where drawings become
living movement clips. They are creating a short premiere for **Flip**, a
playful light creature that copies whatever motion is recorded.

A preview glitch scatters the clip controls. Some actions play forward, some
reverse, and some repeat or undo themselves. Flip keeps missing the final
stage mark in increasingly funny but safe ways.

The child directs the clips, repairs the playback, and then creates a final
motion remix. The finished animation plays on the studio’s giant screen, with
the child choosing its colour and soundtrack style.

### Why a child might care

The child is:

- making a small animation;
- directing a funny character;
- experimenting with forward and reverse motion;
- seeing immediate visual results;
- creating the final remix rather than merely unlocking it.

The goal is understandable without the words integer, multiplier, quotient,
commutative, or distributive.

## 5. Natural world behaviour

The studio has three physical ideas:

### Discrete animation stage

Flip moves on a visible pixel-stage made of whole cells. A valid saved motion
ends on a cell, not halfway between cells. This makes it natural to test
whether an operation stays within the integer grid; the story does not invent
a “whole-number-only” rule after the calculation.

### Movement clip

A clip moves Flip a fixed signed distance from the current position:

- right is one direction;
- left is the opposite direction;
- the stage centre is zero.

The interface initially says **right 3** or **left 3**. `+3` and `−3` appear
only after the child understands the two directions.

### Playback direction

- **Play** runs recorded clips in their original time direction.
- **Undo/Rewind** reverses the effect of completed clips.

The child never sees or hears “negative number of repeats.” After experiencing
the reversal, a signed playback count is introduced as compact notation for
forward plays or undone plays.

### Loop and remix

Clips can be:

- repeated;
- grouped into a sequence;
- reordered when their total signed movement is all that matters;
- split into smaller motion parts;
- recombined while preserving the same final stage position.

The studio naturally exposes multiplication, division, and their properties.

---

## 6. Natural-fit proof

If the integer relationships are removed:

- the editor cannot predict Flip’s final position;
- playing versus undoing the same clip has no consistent result;
- the child cannot reconstruct a missing clip or playback count;
- a remix cannot guarantee that Flip reaches the same final stage mark;
- the premiere cannot be repaired.

The Mathematics is the playback behaviour itself. It is not a question shown
after an unrelated cartoon.

## 7. Story chapter A — The Backward Premiere

### Child promise

> Repair Flip’s mixed-up movement reel and get the character to the centre
> mark for the premiere.

### Starting act

Nova and the child record a simple movement:

1. Flip waves.
2. Flip moves three spaces right.
3. Confetti appears.

Rider from Night Run has sent a funny cameo clip: “Try the reverse button!”

Flip previews the reel, but the control strip is scrambled. The movement plays
left instead of right, then repeats and lands Flip behind the scenery.

- Flip peeks out, unharmed: “Was that the ending?”
- Child: “Not unless the wall is the stage.”
- Nova: “Let’s fix what the reel is doing.”

The child takes the director controls.

### Quest chain

| Quest | Child story action | Natural mathematical relationship |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Record One Move | Move Flip right or left from the centre and save the clip | Signed movement from zero |
| 2. Play the Loop | Run the same clip 1, 2, or 3 times and predict the final stage mark | Positive integer multiplication as repeated signed change |
| 3. Run It Back | Undo completed clips and watch the motion reverse through the same positions | Negative playback direction as inverse/rewind, never negative groups |
| 4. Fix the Four Previews | Combine forward/reverse playback with right/left clips while keeping movement size fixed | All four multiplication sign combinations |
| 5. Find the Missing Clip | Use the final stage mark and one known control to reconstruct the missing movement or playback count | Integer division as inverse multiplication |
| 6. The Impossible Control | Try to reconstruct through a zero divisor and see that no unique inverse clip exists | Division by zero is undefined |
| 7. Premiere Take | Repair a changed reel without colour cues and play it on the real stage | Transfer |

### Formal bridge

Only after the child has played and undone clips:

```text
signed playback count × movement per clip = total movement
```

Examples stay beside the real stage strip:

```text
2 forward plays × 3 left = 6 left
2 undone plays × 3 left = 6 right

2 × (−3) = −6
(−2) × (−3) = +6
```

Nova does not say “two negatives make a positive” as a rule without cause.

### Ending act

The repaired reel lands Flip exactly on the centre mark. The child chooses
Flip’s final pose and launches the first half of the premiere. The audience is
a small group of fictional studio creatures, not ranked peers or evaluators.

The last motion tile starts glowing in several possible arrangements, opening
the remix chapter through curiosity rather than another emergency.

## 8. Story chapter B — Make the Motion Remix

### Child promise

> Turn Flip’s repaired movement into a bigger remix without changing where the
> character finishes.

### Why this is not a property worksheet

The child is editing and performing a clip. Each rearrangement changes the
visible choreography while the final position can remain the same. The
properties describe which edits preserve the total movement and which do not.

### Quest chain

| Quest | Child story action | Natural mathematical relationship |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Swap the Takes | Swap two multiplied movement factors and compare the same total displacement; then contrast subtraction/division edits | Commutative multiplication and its limits |
| 2. Change the Clip Folders | Group three repeated movement factors in different folders and play both versions | Associative multiplication |
| 3. Split the Chorus | Repeat one combined left/right sequence, then repeat its parts separately and overlay the endpoints | Distributive property over addition |
| 4. One Take, No Take, Reverse Take | Play a clip once, zero times, and through a single reverse operator | Multiplicative identity, zero property, and `−1 × a = −a` |
| 5. Count the Reverse Clips | Build a short chain with several negative movement factors and predict final direction from the number of reversals | Sign of a product with multiple integer factors |
| 6. Does the Result Stay on the Integer Grid? | Run edits on the whole-cell pixel stage; addition, subtraction, and multiplication land on cells, while selected divisions visibly land between them and cannot be saved as whole-cell clips | Closure and division counterexamples |
| 7. Final Remix | Assemble one grouped, mixed-operation motion strip that reaches the chosen ending pose | Expression transfer and creative payoff |

### Important limitation

Swapping factors preserves the total product, but it may change how the
animation looks over time. The studio explicitly distinguishes:

- same final numerical movement;
- different possible choreography.

This makes the commutative property precise instead of claiming that two
edited films are identical.

### Ending act

The child chooses the final background, light trail, and music style. Flip
performs the complete remix successfully. Nova and Flip react to the child’s
creative choices before a short visual recap connects playback behaviour to
integer notation.

## 9. Emotional and interaction safeguards

- A bad preview is funny and reversible, never humiliating.
- Flip is never injured or trapped in danger.
- There is no countdown, public score, or “perfect take.”
- The child may scrub the timeline freely and replay consequences.
- The final remix celebrates creation, not speed.
- Nova shares observations but does not narrate every clip.
- Flip and the child both speak and react.
- Formal notation appears on the same timeline after the visual behaviour.
- The studio remains usable without audio through movement traces, direction
  arrows, and captions.

## 11. Architecture decision

- keep **Mountain Rescue** as one focused story chapter;
- retire **Supply Lift Timeline** and **Network Route Rules** as approved
  story titles while preserving their curriculum checklists;
- do not replace them with Replay Realm or GlitchGrid;
- leave the unmatched concepts unassigned until each has a simple natural
  activity;
- preserve all existing Mountain code and assets until future implementation
  decisions are made.

The totals are documentation consequences, not product goals.

## 12. Product decision

Decided:

1. Mountain Rescue owns position, order, addition, and subtraction.
2. Replay Realm and GlitchGrid are rejected.
3. Multiplication, division, and properties remain visible but unassigned.
4. The next deliverable is Mountain Rescue’s connected four-quest story—not
   another replacement-world brainstorm.
5. Do not update production code or delete existing sequel work yet.
