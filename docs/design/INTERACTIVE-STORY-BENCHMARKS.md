# LearnNnjoy Interactive Story Benchmarks

These are the non-negotiable experience gates for every learning chapter.
Nova’s Night Run (Lines and Angles) is the current reference experience.

## 0. Choose the story in the correct order

Every chapter begins with this sequence:

```text
Child fantasy or activity
        ↓
Natural behaviour of that world
        ↓
Mathematical relationship already inside that behaviour
        ↓
Curriculum mapping
```

Never begin with a list of uncovered subtopics and invent an unrelated story
obstacle to hold them.

A proposed story proceeds only when:

- a child can understand and want the activity without mathematical language;
- the world would naturally behave according to the intended relationship;
- the child’s enjoyable story action is also the concept action;
- removing the mathematical relationship would break or materially change the
  story problem;
- Nova can name what the child already experienced instead of explaining why
  Maths has suddenly entered the story.

If a concept does not fit naturally, move it to another story or create another
chapter. The number of chapters is not a target, limit, or success measure.

## 1. The chapter must feel like one complete story

Every chapter needs a clear starting act, seamless quest transitions, and a
real ending act.

### Starting act

The opening must act out:

- where the characters are;
- what they were doing before the problem appeared;
- the incident that creates the problem;
- why Nova and the learner care about solving it;
- why the child’s help is needed.

The chapter must not begin in the middle of an unexplained task or jump from a
single narration line directly into controls.

### Seamless middle

The result of one quest must naturally cause or unlock the next story problem.
Characters, location, stakes, and important objects persist across the
chapter. A quest must not feel like a separate worksheet or a reset of the
same screen with different numbers.

Dialogue, movement, interaction, concept explanation, and transitions should
remain inside the story world. Interface panels must support the scene rather
than making the child feel that the story has stopped and a lesson has begun.

### Ending act

The final successful action must visibly resolve the problem established in
the opening. The world changes, the characters react, and the child sees who
their help affected.

Only after that story payoff may Nova briefly connect the completed actions to
the subject concept. A “Quest complete” card, reward animation, or return
button is not an ending act by itself.

## 2. The child must cause the story action

The primary learning moment must be interactive. The child moves, builds,
tests, steers, arranges, or changes something inside the story world.

A quiz choice, slider, marker, or `Next` button does not pass this gate by
itself. The child’s action must visibly change the world.

## 3. The story action must naturally contain the concept

We choose situations where the subject is already useful. We do not create an
unrelated story and insert mathematics afterward.

The child should first think, “I need to help the characters do this,” not,
“The app has stopped the story to ask me maths.”

## 4. Characters must perform and react

Nova and the learner character must appear during the important action, not
only in opening or closing cards. Their movement, dialogue, expressions, and
reactions must make the consequence feel like part of a living scene.

## 5. Show the consequence before naming the rule

The order is always:

1. story problem;
2. child action;
3. visible consequence;
4. Nova names the pattern in child-friendly language;
5. the idea is tried in a changed situation;
6. a short visual recap connects the action to the formal concept.

Equations, definitions, and formal labels must not replace the visual
discovery or appear before it.

## 6. One chapter is one connected world

Each chapter contains multiple quests inside the same story world. Every quest
advances both the chapter story and the subject understanding.

Later quests must introduce meaningful new actions, consequences, and story
stakes. Reusing the same screen with different numbers does not pass.

## 7. Interaction quality must match the reference

Every quest must be compared with Nova’s Night Run, not merely with an older
quiz screen. Structural parity—four cards, saved progress, audio hooks, or
passing tests—is not experiential parity.

A quest passes only when the child can:

- understand what is happening without reading a long explanation;
- affect the scene through play;
- see why the result happened;
- hear or see characters respond;
- explain the discovered idea in their own words afterward.

## 8. Safety and continuity remain invisible

Home, Previous, exact resume, Journal replay, reduced motion, phone layout,
and reward safety must work without interrupting the story experience.

## Release gate

Do not expand the pattern to the next chapter until one deep quest in the
current chapter has been played in the real browser on desktop and phone and
has passed every benchmark above.

Automated tests prove stability. They do not prove that the experience is
interactive, understandable, or fun.
