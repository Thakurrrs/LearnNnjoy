# Mountain Rescue — Seamless Story Redesign

**Status:** Four-quest connected story implemented. No multiplication,
division, or properties sequel is attached to this chapter.

**Required benchmark:**
[LearnNnjoy Interactive Story Benchmarks](../../design/INTERACTIVE-STORY-BENCHMARKS.md)

## Approved story decisions

1. The emotional setting is a mountain wildlife shelter preparing for aurora
   night. Pip, a playful snow fox, gives the rescue a visible, child-friendly
   reason.
2. The child controls Nova and the learner on a mountain rescue sled. They
   follow the lost pod; they do not control its accidental fall.
3. The four live quests transition directly into one another. The quest map is
available for navigation, resume, and replay but does not interrupt the live
chapter.
4. Mountain Rescue ends after the energy cell returns and the shelter comes
back to life. It is not responsible for covering every integer subtopic.
5. Multiplication, division, and operation properties remain unassigned until
a separate, simple activity is approved. This does not block Mountain Rescue.

## Chapter promise

Integers describe real positions and movements relative to Base Camp at zero.
The child experiences this by completing one continuous rescue rather than by
answering questions about a decorated number line.

## Starting act

### Before the problem

Nova, the learner, the mountain scout, and Pip prepare Ridge Shelter at `+2`
for aurora night. Pip steals a glowing ribbon while Nova tries to decorate.
The child sees the characters enjoying the shelter before anything goes wrong.

Suggested dialogue:

- Nova: “Pip! That goes on the shelter—not your tail!”
- Learner: “He thinks he’s helping.”
- Scout: “Aurora starts soon. We’re almost ready.”

### Inciting incident

The shelter lights flicker, the heater stops, and Pip curls up. The scout
launches a replacement energy cell in a rescue pod from Service Deck at `+3`.

A gust strikes the pod. It visibly passes Ridge Shelter, crosses Base Camp at
`0`, and disappears into the ravine.

Suggested dialogue:

- Learner: “It passed us!”
- Nova: “And Base Camp—it’s still falling!”
- Scout: “Signal lost. The energy cell is inside that pod.”

Nova and the learner jump onto a rescue sled attached to the cliff cable.

## Quest 1 approved storyboard

This is one continuous playable sequence. It does not use explanatory cards
between beats. Dialogue appears in short comic bubbles beside the speaker and
is performed in audio.

### Beat 1 — Aurora preparations

- Ridge Shelter is warm and bright at altitude `+2`.
- Nova hangs a glowing ribbon while the learner carries lanterns.
- Pip steals the loose end, trots around Nova, and makes the decorations spin.
- Nova says, “Pip! The ribbon goes on the shelter—not your tail!”
- The learner laughs: “He thinks he is the decoration.”
- This beat lasts only long enough to establish the characters and give the
  shelter something visible to lose.

### Beat 2 — The storm takes the power

- Wind bends the ribbon and rattles the shelter shutters.
- The lights flicker out, the heater glow disappears, and frost begins to form
  on the window.
- Pip stops playing and curls beside the cold heater.
- The scout says, “The storm drained our energy cell.”
- Nova kneels beside Pip: “We need another one before the aurora arrives.”

### Beat 3 — The replacement pod falls

- The camera moves upward to Service Deck at `+3`.
- The scout launches the replacement energy pod.
- A gust bends its flight path. The pod passes Ridge Shelter at `+2`, crosses
  the clearly marked Base Camp platform at `0`, and disappears into the
  ravine.
- The learner says, “It went past Base Camp!”
- The scout checks a pulsing tracker: “The cell is safe, but the signal is
  fading.”

### Beat 4 — Ask the child for help

- Nova and the learner jump onto a two-person rescue sled clipped to one
  straight cliff cable.
- Nova faces the child: “You steer. I will watch the signal.”
- The child pulls the glowing safety latch to start the sled. The latch is a
  physical story action, not a generic `Next` button.

### Beat 5 — Follow the signal

- The child drags the sled up or down the straight cable. Large Up and Down
  controls provide an equal phone and keyboard alternative.
- Nova and the learner remain visibly attached to the moving sled. Their
  scarves, knees, hands, and sled runners react to motion so they do not float.
- The tracker pulse becomes larger, brighter, and quicker when the child moves
  toward the pod. It softens when moving away.
- Moving the wrong way is allowed. Nova reacts, “The pulse is getting
  quieter—let’s try the other way.” There is no failure screen.
- At Base Camp, the platform beacon wakes and the sled slows briefly.
- The learner says, “Base Camp—our middle marker.”
- The child continues below Base Camp until the signal peaks near `−4`.

### Beat 6 — Find and recover the pod

- A beacon flashes beneath a snowdrift at `−4`.
- The child wipes snow away directly on the scene.
- The pod appears with a bent rotor. Nova and the learner grab its rescue
  handles.
- The child pulls the illuminated recovery strap. Both characters lean,
  slide, and finally pull the pod free.
- The pod opens enough to show the undamaged glowing energy cell.
- The learner says, “We found it! The cell is still glowing.”

### Beat 7 — Name what the child discovered

- The camera retraces the real route instead of cutting to a lesson screen:
  Service Deck `+3`, Base Camp `0`, Ridge Shelter `+2`, and the pod at `−4`.
- Nova says, “Base Camp is our zero spot. Places above it use a plus. Places
  below it use a minus.”
- The compact trail `+3 → 0 → −4` appears beside the actual locations.
- This is the first time the terms positive and negative are introduced.

### Beat 8 — Try the idea once

- Two rescue flags appear in the child’s hands: a shelter flag and a pod flag.
- The child places the shelter flag at `+2` and the pod flag at `−4`.
- Each correct placement makes its real location glow and the characters
  react. An incorrect placement simply lets the flag bounce gently back.
- Nova says, “Plus two is above our zero spot. Minus four is below it.”

### Beat 9 — Flow directly into Quest 2

- Before a completion card appears, the scout calls through the radio:
  “The storm knocked out the altitude beacons. The winch cannot find you.”
- The camera pulls back to reveal the dark chain of beacons continuing up the
  same cliff.
- Nova lifts the beacon kit from the sled: “Then we light a path home.”
- The learner clips the pod to the sled.
- Quest 2 begins in this exact location with the same characters and pod.

### Quest 1 acceptance checks

- The opening can be understood without reading narration.
- The shelter and Pip make the lost power cell matter.
- The pod’s fall is watched; it is not controlled by the child.
- The child controls the rescue team and changes the world through steering,
  brushing snow, pulling the pod, and placing flags.
- Motion remains on straight paths so curved movement does not confuse the
  integer idea.
- Positive, zero, and negative are named only after the child finds the pod.
- Characters speak, move, and react during the action.
- Quest 2 starts from Quest 1’s consequence without showing the quest map.

## Quest 1 — Chase the Lost Signal

### Story problem

The pod and its energy cell are missing below Base Camp. Pip and the shelter
need the cell back.

### Child action

The child controls the rescue sled carrying Nova and the learner:

- drag along the straight cliff cable;
- use large Up and Down controls as a phone and accessibility equivalent;
- pass physical altitude signs embedded in the ledges;
- follow tracker pulses that strengthen near the pod;
- brush away the snowdrift at `−4`;
- help Nova and the learner pull the pod free.

Moving away from the signal makes it quieter. It is discovery feedback, not a
red failure state.

### Visible consequence

The camera and environment travel with the team. Base Camp at `0` lights up as
they cross it. Below Base Camp, the scenery becomes a sheltered ravine. At
`−4`, the pod beacon flashes beneath the snow.

The pod opens and the energy cell is visibly safe, but its rotor is bent.

### Concept reveal and transfer

Only after the pod is found:

- the camera retraces Service Deck `+3`, Base Camp `0`, and the pod at `−4`;
- Nova names positions above zero as positive and below zero as negative;
- the compact route `+3 → 0 → −4` appears;
- the child places one rescue flag at Ridge Shelter `+2` and another at the
  found pod `−4`.

### Seamless transition

The scout reports that the storm also disabled the altitude beacons needed by
the rescue winch. The camera pulls back from the pod to reveal the dark beacon
chain.

Nova picks up a beacon kit:

> “If we relight them from the valley upward, the winch can see us.”

Quest 2 begins without returning to the quest map.

## Quest 2 — Relight the Beacon Chain

### Story problem

The winch cannot safely reach the pod because its altitude beacons are dark
and out of sequence.

### Child action

The child moves the same rescue team through actual mountain locations:

- start beside the pod in the ravine;
- choose the next higher dark beacon;
- climb, jump, or zip-line along the selected route;
- connect each beacon with a glowing rescue cable;
- see the team physically arrive before choosing the next location.

Choosing a lower location is allowed. The team travels there, the chain
flickers, and Nova observes that the route moved downward. The child can then
continue without losing progress.

### Visible consequence

Every activated beacon lights a larger portion of the cliff. The completed
chain visibly runs from the lowest position to the highest.

### Concept reveal and transfer

After the route is built, Nova names the pattern: higher signed positions are
greater, and lower signed positions are smaller. A short second route uses
changed beacon positions to prove that negative positions also have an order.

### Seamless transition

The final beacon wakes the winch. Its cable descends toward the pod, but a
fresh wind burst catches the suspended pod and cable.

The movement immediately starts Quest 3.

## Quest 3 — Ride the Storm

### Story problem

The pod is now hanging from the rescue cable while successive gusts push it
up and down the cliff.

### Child action

The child performs every movement:

- the scout calls one gust direction and distance;
- glowing anchor points appear one level at a time;
- the child drags the pod and cable through those anchors;
- the cable bends, the pod swings, and the characters brace against the wind;
- the final position after one gust becomes the starting position for the next.

There is no answer selection. The child enacts the running movement.

### Visible consequence

Each gust changes the pod’s real cliff position. Earlier positions remain as a
fading trail, so the child can see the complete movement history.

### Concept reveal and transfer

Nova names upward movement as adding a positive amount and downward movement
as adding a negative amount. A shorter changed gust sequence lets the child
reuse the same movement idea.

### Seamless transition

The storm calms with the pod below Ridge Shelter. The cable is tangled and the
empty winch hook remains at the shelter.

The scout says:

> “Send the hook down to the pod, attach it, then reverse the winch.”

Quest 4 begins in the same scene.

## Quest 4 — Bring the Power Home

### Story problem

The pod is safe but still below the shelter. The hook must travel down to the
pod and then reverse direction with the pod attached.

### Child action

The child operates a visible winch wheel:

1. turn the wheel to lower the empty hook;
2. watch the cable extend through each altitude;
3. help Nova and the learner attach the hook;
4. reverse the wheel;
5. lift the pod back to Ridge Shelter at `+2`.

### Visible consequence

The cable length, hook, pod, characters, and altitude signs all change
together. The downward and upward routes are visibly the same path in opposite
directions.

### Concept reveal

Only after the pod reaches the shelter does Nova name subtraction and inverse
movement. The formal route confirms the action that already happened.

## Ending act

The learner opens the pod and hands the energy cell to the scout. Light travels
through the shelter, the heaters glow, Pip jumps up, and the storm clouds part
to reveal the aurora.

Nova, the learner, the scout, and Pip react together. The chapter then briefly
replays:

1. positions above and below Base Camp;
2. the ordered beacon chain;
3. the accumulated storm movements;
4. the reversed winch route.

Nova’s explanation follows the story resolution. A reward or “Chapter
complete” control appears only after this ending act.

## Live-flow rule

Quest completion updates saved progress but does not open the quest map.
Instead, the ending beat of the current quest becomes the opening beat of the
next.

Home, Previous, the optional quest map, and Journal replay continue to preserve
the exact live scene and interaction state.

## Explicit removals from the current version

- No above/below prediction before the pod falls.
- The child does not drag the lost pod during the search.
- A number line is not the primary visual environment.
- Marker taps alone are not the primary Quest 2 interaction.
- A “release gust” button alone is not the primary Quest 3 interaction.
- Quest completion does not interrupt the live story with the chapter map.
- The chapter does not end with only a recap panel and completion button.
