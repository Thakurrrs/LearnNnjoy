**Product:** LearnNnjoy
**Audience:** Grade 7 students
**Curriculum reference:** NCERT *Ganita Prakash*, Grade 7, Chapter 5 — [*Parallel and Intersecting Lines*](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/gegp105.pdf)
**Vertical-slice quest:** *Nova's Night Run — Crossing Rails*

## 1. Recommendation

Keep the **Skatepark Architect** world, but replace the current “build an exact 60° ramp, solve a triangle angle, explain the label” sequence with a short interactive animated episode called **Nova's Night Run**.

The current sequence combines three different lessons:

1. An angle as an amount of turn.
2. A triangle's angles adding to 180°.
3. Interpreting a 60° construction label.

That makes the story broad but conceptually shallow. It also mixes the current NCERT line-relationships chapter with the separate triangle chapter.

The revised experience will make Skatepark Architect the setting for a small **chapter questline**, not pretend that one five-scene story teaches the full chapter:

| Quest | Central mental model | Curriculum ideas |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Trail Meet | A straight trail keeps one direction; two straight trails may meet at one point | Straight lines and an intersection point |
| 2. Crossing Rails | When two straight lines cross, the four angles are connected | Vertically opposite angles and linear pairs |
| 3. Rails That Never Meet | Parallel lines keep their direction; perpendicular lines meet squarely | Parallel and perpendicular lines |
| 4. The Crossing Beam | A transversal creates repeatable angle patterns across parallel lines | Transversal and corresponding angles |

Quest 1 is the verified interaction pattern: Nova demonstrates one straight trail,
parks safely with the skateboard, and the child chooses any angle for a second
straight trail. The child sees either a glowing intersection point or two separate
trails; neither result is treated as failure. In production this becomes the first
event of the chapter questline, not evidence that the chapter is complete.

Quest 2 is the next deep implementation target. It gives the team one complete,
child-testable angle-relationship story before expanding the chapter or copying
the pattern into the other Grade 7 worlds.

Skateboard jumps and tricks may supply story stakes or a finale, but they are not
the mathematical explanation for this chapter. A ramp's take-off angle can teach
tilt or turn in a different concept journey; it does not naturally demonstrate
vertically opposite angles, linear pairs or corresponding angles. Track and rail
relationships remain the visible mathematical objects here.

The child-facing premise is deliberately simpler than the learning plan:

> Nova and the child are about to open a glowing night skate course. A crossing in the track loses its light pattern and the next section will not open. The child moves the rails, discovers the hidden pattern and lights the course so they can ride it together.

The child should think, “I am helping Nova light the skate course.” The curriculum structure remains invisible behind that goal.

The later **Skybridge Survey** adventure should own triangle construction, side relationships and angle-sum concepts.

## 2. Quest 2 — Nova's Night Run: Crossing Rails

### Concept Story Blueprint

**Concept truth**
When two straight lines cross, they form four angles. Opposite angles are equal. Two neighbouring angles that sit on one straight line combine to make a straight angle.

**Common misunderstandings**

- The four angles at a crossing are unrelated.
- Angles that are close together must be equal.
- A longer arm creates a larger angle.
- Opposite angles only look equal in a specially drawn diagram.
- Mathematical vocabulary is the rule itself rather than a name for a visible relationship.

**What visibly changes**
One safety rail rotates around a fixed crossing point. All four angle openings change together: one opposite pair grows while the other opposite pair shrinks.

**Why Nova needs help**
The night course will not open because the four lights around a crossed rail have lost their pattern. Nova can see that the lights move when the rail turns but cannot work out which corners belong together.

**What the child avatar does**
The avatar tests the dark track, rotates the crossed rail, carries a glowing angle tracing to its twin, joins two neighbouring lights into a straight path and completes the opening ride with Nova.

**What the child should notice**

1. A crossing creates four angle openings.
2. Opposite openings change together and always match.
3. Neighbouring openings along a straight rail fill a half-turn together.
4. These relationships survive when the crossing is rotated or redrawn.

**When the concept is named**
Nova introduces “vertically opposite angles” only after the child has overlaid one opening on its opposite and seen the fit. “Linear pair” appears after two neighbouring openings animate into one straight half-turn.

**Where the child sees it again**
A differently rotated crossing appears during the opening ride. The child finds the same two relationships to illuminate the final track section.

**How the story resolves**
The final track section lights up. Nova and the avatar ride the whole course together, then Nova gives a brief visual recap of the two discoveries.

## 3. Five-event story

The five events map to the existing saved-event structure, but each event has one learning job. The story is experienced as one continuous night run rather than five separate lesson cards.

### Event 1 — Lights Out!

**Learning moment:** Invite and notice.

**Visible story**

- Nova and the avatar roll onto the glowing start platform.
- Nova taps the launch light; the course flickers and goes dark.
- A crossed rail turns slightly by itself.
- Four corner lights around the crossing flash out of rhythm.
- Nova nearly rolls into a padded barrier, then looks back at the crossing.

**Nova line**

> “Uh… that crossing just knocked out the whole track.”

**Child action**

Tap the flashing crossing. The four corner lights appear one at a time.

**Feedback**

- Each tap wakes a different corner light.
- Nova follows each light with their gaze and board.
- No terms, degrees or answer choices appear yet.

**End-state evidence**

The child has noticed that one crossing makes four angles.

### Event 2 — Make It Move

**Learning moment:** Predict and manipulate.

**Visible story**

- One rail stays fixed.
- The child drags the other rail around the joint.
- The four wedges expand and contract continuously.
- The avatar braces the rail while Nova balances on their board.

**Nova line**

> “Turn it—let's see what moves with it.”

**Child action**

Rotate the rail freely through a safe range. Before the first drag, the child may tap which opposite opening they expect to grow.

**Feedback**

- The selected opening is treated as a prediction, not a scored answer.
- The world immediately shows what grows and what shrinks.
- Nova reacts with curiosity if the result differs from the prediction: “Wait—look which light moved with it!”
- The experience does not stop at a target degree.

**End-state evidence**

The child has experienced the four angles as one connected system.

### Event 3 — Find the Twin

**Learning moment:** Make meaning.

**Visible story**

- A glowing tracing appears over one corner.
- The child drags the tracing across the intersection.
- It snaps onto the opposite angle and fits exactly.
- The rail can still be rotated; both opposite tracings continue to match.

**Nova line before naming**

> “Wait… that corner has a twin!”

**Concept reveal**

> “Maths calls them vertically opposite angles. Big name, simple pattern.”

An unobtrusive visual label appears beside the matched pair:

> **Vertically opposite angles** · matching corners across a crossing

**Child action**

Try the tracing at one neighbouring opening and then at the opposite opening.

**Feedback**

- A neighbouring opening visibly does not fit; this is shown as information, not failure.
- The opposite opening gives a soft lock animation and Nova's surprised pose.
- A short optional “Show me why” replay reveals the matching movement.

**End-state evidence**

The child can recognise the opposite partner without using angle measurements.

### Event 4 — Light the Straight Run

**Learning moment:** Connect the second relationship.

**Visible story**

- Two neighbouring light pieces lift from the joint.
- As the child slides them together, their outer arms align into one glowing straight rail.
- The combined light forms a semicircle.
- Only then does the `180°` label appear.

**Nova line**

> “Slide these together—boom, one straight line!”

**Concept reveal**

> “That side-by-side match is called a linear pair.”

An unobtrusive visual label appears beside the joined pieces:

> **Linear pair** · side-by-side angles making a straight line

**Child action**

Choose either neighbour beside a highlighted angle, then join the two pieces into the straight rail.

**Feedback**

- Both neighbours are valid; the activity avoids a fake single-answer path.
- The half-turn animation is the explanation.
- Numerical calculation is not required.

**End-state evidence**

The child sees why neighbouring angles on a straight line total 180°, instead of memorising the statement.

### Event 5 — Catch the Pattern

**Learning moment:** See it again and discover.

**Visible story**

- Nova and the avatar begin the opening ride together.
- A second crossing appears ahead at a different rotation and the track stops lighting.
- One corner flashes; no degree value is shown.
- The child first finds its opposite twin, then joins a valid side-by-side pair.
- Each discovery illuminates another stretch of track.

**Nova line**

> “Same pattern, new crossing. You got it!”

**Child action**

Use the relationships in a differently oriented crossing.

**Finale**

- The final track section lights up.
- Nova and the avatar ride from launch to landing together.
- Nova hops once, points back to a clean replay of both discoveries, and says:

> “We cracked it! Across-the-crossing corners match. Side-by-side ones make a straight line.”

The spoken recap stays natural while two compact labels appear:

```text
VERTICALLY OPPOSITE ANGLES
Matching angles across a crossing

LINEAR PAIR · 180°
Side-by-side angles making a straight line
```

The child may tap **Show me why** for a ten-second visual replay. The deeper explanation is never required to finish the adventure.

**End-state evidence**

The child transfers the mental model to a new orientation without solving for an unknown angle.

## 4. Character and animation direction

### Nova's role

Nova is not a talking hint card. Nova:

- enters with the real story problem;
- looks at the exact angle currently changing;
- makes a prediction feel safe;
- reacts to the visible relationship;
- names the concept after discovery;
- celebrates the child's observation, not speed or correctness.

Nova sounds like a clever Grade 7 teammate—not a teacher performing friendliness.

### Nova dialogue rules

- Use 4–10 spoken words most of the time.
- React emotionally before explaining.
- Use contractions and everyday language.
- Let Nova wonder, notice and occasionally be surprised.
- Avoid baby talk and fake teenage slang.
- Do not speak a formal definition before the child sees the relationship.
- Introduce only one official Maths term at a time.
- Keep the official term visible after Nova's spoken line ends.
- Do not praise every tap; reserve celebration for a real discovery.
- Write for performance, including natural pauses such as “Wait…” and “Oh!”

### Show, name, reuse, recap

Nova's teaching pattern is:

1. **Show it:** the child causes the mathematical relationship to become visible.
2. **Name it:** Nova connects one friendly sentence to the official term.
3. **Reuse it:** the child recognises the relationship in a new orientation.
4. **Recap it:** Nova replays the discovery visually after the story succeeds.

The end recap lasts 15–20 seconds. It summarizes what the child already experienced; it does not introduce a new lecture.

### Avatar's role

The selected avatar:

- tests the unstable platform;
- holds and rotates the rail;
- carries the transparent tracing;
- locks the repaired support;
- performs the successful final ride.

The avatar does not stand beside the activity as decoration.

### Motion budget

- Opening: 6–8 seconds, skippable.
- Immediate reactions: 0.4–0.9 seconds.
- Concept reveal: up to 1.5 seconds.
- Finale: 2–3 seconds, skippable on replay.
- Reduced motion: pose, focus ring and colour change replace movement.

### Text budget

- One Nova sentence at a time.
- Normal line length: 6–12 words.
- Maximum line length: 16 words.
- One direct action instruction.
- Vocabulary appears only after the visible experience.
- “Why?” contains the deeper explanation and is optional.
- Formal labels may remain on screen longer than Nova's spoken line.
- The child can replay Nova's line or continue without audio.

## 5. Interaction rules

1. Do not begin with “make the angle exactly 60°.”
2. Do not lock progress behind repeated precise slider placement.
3. Treat a prediction as a hypothesis, not a graded answer.
4. Let a non-matching tracing visibly fail to fit without red error styling.
5. Keep the mathematical object and the child's control visible together on a 375 × 812 screen.
6. Preserve the state of the rotated rail, prediction and discovered relationships when the child goes Home or to the Journal.
7. In replay mode, let the child manipulate every event without changing rewards or live progress.
8. Remove hand-camera control from this child-test path.

## 6. What this story deliberately does not teach

- Triangle angle sum.
- Triangle construction.
- Finding an unknown angle from two numerical values.
- Measuring an angle accurately with a protractor.
- All parallel-line and transversal relationships.
- Exam-style naming drills.

Those are separate concept journeys. Excluding them is necessary for depth, not a reduction in the planned Grade 7 curriculum.

## 7. Chapter continuation

### Quest 1 — Trail Meet

**Mental model:** A child-controlled skateboard draws one straight trail from a
fixed start in any chosen direction. Nova's straight trail remains visible.

The child may create:

- an intersection, where the two straight trails meet at one glowing point; or
- a separate route, where the visible trails do not meet.

The route is always a single straight stroke. A curved trail is not allowed in
this quest because it introduces a different mathematical object. A route that
does not meet Nova's trail is not automatically labelled “parallel”; the finite
picture only proves that the visible trails have not met.

### Quest 3 — Rails That Never Meet

**Mental model:** Parallel lines keep the same direction and do not meet when extended; perpendicular lines meet at four right angles.

The child extends two apparently separate rails to reveal that they eventually collide, rotates one until the spacing remains stable, and squares a safety gate using a visible right-angle corner.

### Quest 4 — The Crossing Beam

**Mental model:** A transversal creates angle positions that repeat across parallel lines; matching corresponding angles gives evidence that the lines are parallel.

The child places one diagonal support across two rails, traces a corresponding angle to the second intersection, disturbs one rail to break the match, and restores the parallel structure by making the angle pattern fit again.

These quests should receive their own five-event stories rather than being compressed into *Crossing Rails*.

## 8. Learning evidence

The vertical slice is successful when a child can:

- point to all four angles formed by two intersecting lines;
- find the angle opposite a selected angle;
- show, using the tracing, that opposite angles match;
- show how two neighbours form a straight half-turn;
- recognise both relationships in a differently oriented crossing;
- explain the discovery in their own words without needing the official terms.

It is not necessary for the child to:

- calculate an unknown degree value;
- repeat a definition word for word;
- finish quickly;
- earn a perfect score.

## 9. Child-test prompts

Use observation and open prompts rather than leading questions:

1. “What do you think will move when you turn this rail?”
2. “Show me which corner has a twin.”
3. “How did you know those two matched?”
4. “What happens when these neighbours join?”
5. “Can you find the same pattern on the new support?”
6. “What would you tell another child about crossing lines?”

Record hesitation, unsolicited replay, explanation quality and where adult help was required. Do not use likes alone as evidence of learning.
