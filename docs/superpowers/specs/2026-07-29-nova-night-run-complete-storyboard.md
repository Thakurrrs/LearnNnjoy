## 1. Final recommendation

Keep **Nova’s Night Run** as the story for lines and angles.

The skate course is not decoration around Mathematics. Its straight trails,
crossings, parallel rails, perpendicular service lanes, and crossing beams are
the mathematical objects. The child learns by riding, rotating, tracing,
joining, extending, and rebuilding them.

The complete chapter contains eight quests in three resumable acts:

1. **Act 1 — The Crossing**
   - Trail Meet
   - Crossing Rails
2. **Act 2 — The Parallel Course**
   - Rails That Never Meet
   - Same Corner Lights
3. **Act 3 — The Full Night Run**
   - Zigzag Lights
   - Inside Together
   - Reverse Check
   - Opening Ride

The child never sees the chapter as eight lessons. They experience one night
course opening, with satisfying pause points after Acts 1 and 2.

The child should first want to open the course. Formal names arrive only after

## 3. Concept contract

### Prerequisites

The child can:

- recognise a point, line segment, ray, and straight line at a basic level;
- recognise a right angle and a straight angle;
- understand an angle as an opening or turn;
- add and subtract within 180.

Prerequisites are refreshed visually when needed. They are not retaught as a
separate quiz.

### Core relationships

- Two distinct straight lines in one plane meet at no more than one point.
- Two intersecting lines create four related angles.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal.
- Each linear pair totals 180°.
- Perpendicular lines intersect at right angles.
- Parallel lines are coplanar and never meet however far they extend.
- A transversal creates two sets of four angle positions.
- Corresponding and alternate angles are equal when a transversal intersects
  parallel lines.
- Same-side interior angles total 180°.
- The corresponding-angle relation can be used in reverse to construct or
  verify parallel lines.
- The relationships survive rotation, changed orientation, visual illusion,
  and imperfect drawing.

### Deliberate exclusions

- triangle angle sum and triangle theorems;
- triangle construction;
- skateboard jump physics as geometrical proof;
- precise protractor technique as the main learning objective;
- timed calculations or repeated exam-style angle drills;
- curved paths being described as straight lines.

Triangle concepts remain in Triangle Trail. Precise constructions using a
compass and straightedge remain in Foldspace Workshop.

## 5. Complete story arc

### Before the problem

The night course is closed for its final lighting test. Nova, the selected
child avatar, and Rider warm up on the start deck.

- Rider rolls past: “One last test lap?”
- Child: “Only if Nova keeps up.”
- Nova, fastening their helmet: “Rude. Fair. Let’s light it.”

The child taps the start rail. Nova and Rider push off on two glowing practice
lanes. Wheels stay attached to the ground; boards tilt with the body and cast
contact shadows.

### Inciting incident

The central switching rail turns unexpectedly. Its four corner lights flash in
the wrong order. The course ahead goes dark and the opening gate locks.

Nova brakes and steps safely to the side, holding the skateboard. Rider crosses
the open space without colliding.

- Rider: “The crossing forgot its pattern.”
- Child: “And it took the whole course with it.”
- Nova: “Then we teach it the route again.”

The problem is understandable without narration: the course cannot open until
its line-and-angle light patterns are rebuilt.

### Story continuity

Each repaired section sends light into the next dark section:

```text
test trails
  → crossed rail lights
  → parallel exit rails
  → transversal corner lights
  → zigzag route
  → inside charging gate
  → illusion tunnel
  → final opening ride
```

Characters, boards, repaired light traces, and the child’s discoveries persist
across quests.

## 6. Act 1 — The Crossing

## Quest 1 — Trail Meet

### Immediate story problem

The controller has lost the two original test trails that tell it where the
central crossing should be.

### Child action

1. Nova rides one straight star trail and parks safely at its end.
2. A guide-star lane appears from the child’s start point.
3. The child chooses any direction and rides one straight trail.
4. The child may produce an intersection or a visibly separate pair of trail
   segments. Neither is treated as failure.
5. The child extends the thin centre traces to see whether and where the ideal
   lines meet.

The board moves on the line the child creates. It does not float or follow a
curved drag history.

### Same-plane beat

Rider briefly rolls across an overhead bridge.

- Rider: “Mine doesn’t meet either. Parallel?”
- Nova: “Different floor. We compare lines on one flat surface.”

The bridge fades from the mathematical focus. The top-down course plane
remains.

### Visible consequence

- If the traces meet, one intersection point glows.
- If the visible segments do not meet, extending them may reveal a later
  intersection.
- Two distinct straight traces never form two separate intersection points.

### Concept reveal

Nova reacts before naming:

> “Two straight routes. One meeting spot.”

Compact labels attach to the real objects:

- `line segment` beside the ridden portion;
- `line` beside its continuing centre trace;
- `intersection point` at the glow.

### Evidence

The child can extend or rotate a changed pair and indicate whether they meet,
without relying only on the original visible segment lengths.

### Seamless transition

The intersection point sends light into the physical crossed rail, but the four
corner lights around it pulse out of sync.

- Rider: “We found the meeting spot.”
- Nova: “Now its corners are arguing.”

Quest 2 starts at the same crossing.

## Quest 2 — Crossing Rails

### Immediate story problem

The central crossing cannot choose which corner lights belong together.

### Child action and sequence

1. Tap the crossing and wake all four corner lights.
2. Predict one corner that will grow when the movable rail turns.
3. Rotate one rail around the fixed intersection.
4. Watch one opposite pair grow while the other pair shrinks.
5. Drag a transparent corner tracing first to a neighbour and then to its
   opposite twin.
6. Join either valid neighbouring pair into a straight half-turn.

Predictions are hypotheses, never scored answers.

### Visible consequence

- Opposite traces continue to match at every safe rotation.
- A neighbouring trace visibly does not fit.
- Two neighbouring angles along one rail form a glowing semicircle.
- `180°` appears only after that straight half-turn exists.

### Measurement-versus-reasoning beat

The thick illustrated rail edges make a quick measurement look slightly
unequal. The child taps **Show the centre lines**. Thin ideal lines appear and
the exact relation becomes clear.

- Child: “The paint made it look off.”
- Nova: “The drawing can wobble. The line relationship doesn’t.”

This introduces the difference between visual measurement and mathematical
reasoning without turning the story into a lecture.

### Concept reveal

After the tracing fits:

> “Across-the-crossing corners match. They’re vertically opposite angles.”

After the neighbouring pieces form a straight line:

> “Side-by-side angles are adjacent. On one straight line, they’re a linear
> pair.”

The child first sees **adjacent angles** as the general side-by-side
relationship. **Linear pair** is then attached only to the adjacent pair whose
outer arms form a straight line.

### Evidence

At a differently rotated crossing, the child:

- finds the opposite partner;
- chooses either valid neighbour;
- explains or demonstrates why the pair is a straight half-turn.

### Seamless transition and Act 1 rest point

The central crossing lights. Two exit rails illuminate, but their spacing
narrows in the distance. Rider looks down the course.

- Rider: “Those rails look separate.”
- Child: “For now.”
- Nova: “Let’s see where they’re really heading.”

The child may **Continue the run** or **Rest at the crossing**. Resume returns
to this exact lit crossing with all traces preserved.

## 7. Act 2 — The Parallel Course

## Quest 3 — Rails That Never Meet

### Immediate story problem

The exit rails appear separate near the crossing but collide farther down,
blocking the service lane.

### Child action

1. Extend both rail centre lines until their hidden collision appears.
2. Rotate one rail while watching spacing markers at several locations.
3. Lock the rail when the spacing stays stable and the extended lines never
   meet.
4. Square the service crossing by turning its gate until all four corner
   lights become right angles.
5. Split one right-angle light into two freely sized pieces and recombine them.

### Visible consequence

- Merely separate rails may intersect when extended.
- Parallel rails remain apart across the same flat course.
- The service gate meets both rails at 90°.
- Two parts of one right angle total 90°, even when their sizes differ.

### Notation reveal

After the child has built the relationships:

- matching arrow marks appear on the parallel rails;
- a square corner appears at the perpendicular service crossing;
- the symbols stay attached while the course rotates.

### Concept reveal

- Nova: “Same flat course. Extend them forever—they still don’t meet.”
- Rider: “And this gate meets them square.”
- Nova: “Parallel rails. Perpendicular crossing.”

For the split right angle:

> “Two openings, one right-angle corner. Together they make ninety.”

The formal term **complementary angles** appears beside the same two light
pieces.

### Evidence

The child repairs a changed diagonal pair that initially looks parallel but
converges when extended, then marks the parallel and perpendicular relations.

### Seamless transition

The restored rails wake a crossing beam that passes over both. The beam creates
two groups of four corner lights, but only the first group knows its pattern.

- Rider: “Eight corners. Four are still asleep.”
- Nova: “Maybe the first crossing can teach the second.”

---

## Quest 4 — Same Corner Lights

### Immediate story problem

The second crossing on the beam cannot identify the corner that occupies the
same relative position as the first.

### Child action

1. Rider travels along the crossing beam so its role is physically clear.
2. The child rotates the view and identifies the two groups of four angles.
3. The word **transversal** appears only after the beam has crossed both rails.
4. The child lifts a transparent corner trace from the first intersection.
5. The trace is carried to the same relative corner at the second intersection.
6. The child disturbs one rail and watches the match break, then restores the
   parallel relationship and sees the trace fit again.

### Visible consequence

Corresponding is a position relationship, not “two angles with the same
colour.” Colour cues initially indicate top/bottom and left/right locations,
then fade.

### Concept reveal

- Child: “It’s the same corner on the next crossing.”
- Nova: “Exactly. Those are corresponding angles.”
- Rider, after the rail shifts: “And they only match when these rails stay
  parallel.”

### Evidence

On a rotated pair of parallel rails, the child finds a corresponding partner
without relying on colour and shows how changing parallelism changes the fit.

### Seamless transition and Act 2 rest point

The corresponding corner lights send a zigzag pulse through the next tunnel.
Two inside and two outside routes flash.

- Nova: “The light just crossed sides.”
- Child: “Inside first. Then outside?”
- Rider: “Race you to the zigzag.”

The child may **Follow the light** or **Rest at the beam**. Resume preserves
rail position and the discovered corresponding trace.

## 8. Act 3 — The Full Night Run

## Quest 5 — Zigzag Lights

### Immediate story problem

The tunnel’s alternating light pairs are dark, so the boards cannot read the
safe route through it.

### Child action

1. Identify the interior region between the rails and the exterior regions
   outside them.
2. Carry an angle trace across the transversal and to the opposite side.
3. Test an inside zigzag partner.
4. In a second, clearly separated beat, test an outside zigzag partner.
5. Rotate the entire tunnel and repeat after location colours fade.

### Visible consequence

The matching zigzag pair lights together when the rails are parallel. An
inside/outside mismatch does not snap into place; it remains available for
comparison.

### Concept reveal

- Rider: “Across the beam. Other side.”
- Child: “The inside zigzag matches.”
- Nova: “Alternate interior angles.”

Only after that idea is secure does Nova label the outside pair as
**alternate exterior angles** for compatibility coverage.

### Evidence

The child finds an alternate interior pair after rotation and explains the
location pattern through gesture or words.

### Seamless transition

Both zigzag pulses arrive at a charging gate, but the two inside lights on one
side will not form the straight charging bar.

- Nova: “These two don’t match.”
- Child: “Maybe they aren’t supposed to.”

---

## Quest 6 — Inside Together

### Immediate story problem

The charging gate needs two same-side interior lights to combine into one
straight half-turn.

### Child action

1. Choose the two interior angles on one side of the transversal.
2. Lift and rotate their light pieces without changing their size.
3. Join them into one straight bar.
4. Repeat on the opposite side with a changed transversal angle.

### Visible consequence

The two angles are generally not equal, but together they fill exactly 180°.
When one grows, the other shrinks by the same amount.

### Concept reveal

- Child: “Different sizes. Still one straight bar.”
- Nova: “Same-side interior angles add to 180°.”
- Rider: “So matching wasn’t the goal this time.”

The compact term **supplementary angles** appears beside the completed 180°
bar. Nova does not require the child to repeat the word before continuing.

### Evidence

The child rejects “these two should be equal” and demonstrates the straight
sum at a changed crossing.

### Seamless transition

The charging gate powers an illusion tunnel. Patterned wall lights make its
rails appear tilted even though the controller labels them parallel.

- Rider: “My eyes say they’re bending.”
- Nova: “Then we don’t trust our eyes alone.”

---

## Quest 7 — Reverse Check

### Immediate story problem

The illusion tunnel cannot open until the child verifies the rails and rebuilds
one parallel guide through a marked service point.

### Child action

1. Ignore the misleading wall pattern and place a transversal.
2. Choose one corresponding or alternate relationship as the test.
3. Trace the relationship and see whether it matches.
4. Move the faulty rail through a marked point until the chosen relationship
   becomes equal.
5. Extend both rails and verify that they do not meet.
6. Add the parallel arrow notation to approve the tunnel.

Only one converse is active at a time. The child does not face a wall of angle
rules.

### Visible consequence

- A visual appearance can mislead.
- Equal corresponding angles make the constructed rails parallel.
- A line through a required point can be built from a verified relation, not
  by eyeballing it.

### Concept reveal

- Nova: “We used the angle match backwards.”
- Child: “Make these corresponding corners equal…”
- Rider: “…and the rail stays parallel.”

### Evidence

With a different point and orientation, the child constructs another parallel
line and justifies it using the visible relationship.

### Seamless transition

The illusion pattern collapses into a clean light grid. The full course opens
except for one final turn whose corner value is missing.

- Rider: “One dark corner between us and opening night.”
- Nova: “No new rule. Just our whole run together.”

---

## Quest 8 — Opening Ride

### Immediate story problem

The final turn connects two course sections drawn in different orientations.
The light controller knows one angle but cannot carry it through the route.

### Child action

1. Nova rides the first known section and leaves a `65°` light trace.
2. The child uses corresponding and vertically opposite relationships to carry
   that value through the parallel crossings.
3. The child joins a linear pair to reveal the supplementary `115°` turn.
4. At a bend where the direct relationship is hidden, the child draws one
   temporary parallel guide through the marked point.
5. The child rides the completed path; Rider follows, then Nova rejoins.

The child builds the chain through tracing and joining. They do not select
`115°` from answer cards.

### Visible consequence

Each justified relationship lights the next physical section of track. The
formal chain stays attached to the real crossings:

```text
corresponding → opposite → straight pair
     65°          65°          115°
```

The auxiliary line fades after showing why the final turn connects.

### Concept reveal

Nova does not lecture. The characters recap the run together:

- Rider: “Same corner carried the light.”
- Child: “Across the crossing kept it.”
- Nova: “And the straight pair finished the turn.”

### Final evidence

In one unfamiliar orientation, the child:

- identifies the needed relationship;
- constructs or uses one parallel guide;
- carries a known angle through a reasoning chain;
- explains why the final value follows.

### Ending act

The final trail lights from the starting deck to the finish bowl. Nova, the
child, and Rider ride the course together:

1. wheels follow the exact trails;
2. each repaired relationship flashes as they pass;
3. Nova parks safely beside the final crossing, holding the board;
4. the child controls the final light colour and launches the closing sweep;
5. the course sign changes from **TEST RUN** to **NIGHT RUN OPEN**.

Rider offers a fist bump. Nova looks back at the lit track.

- Rider: “You didn’t just ride it. You rebuilt it.”
- Nova: “Opening lap tomorrow?”
- Child response choice: “Definitely.” / “Only if you keep up.”

The response changes only the closing reaction, not rewards or learning
status.

After the emotional payoff, a 15-second optional replay shows:

- across-the-crossing match;
- straight-pair sum;
- repeating angles across parallel rails;
- the final reasoning chain.

No new concept appears in the recap.

## 9. Misconception map

| Misconception | Safe story probe | Informative feedback |
|---|---|---|
| A finite segment that does not meet another is automatically parallel | Extend both centre traces in Quest 1 | A later intersection glows; no red failure screen |
| Lines on different planes that never meet are parallel | Rider’s overhead bridge | Top-down plane and height separation become visible |
| Longer angle arms create a larger angle | Change rail length without changing opening in Quest 2 | Corner tracing remains unchanged |
| Nearby angles must match | Try the opposite tracing on a neighbour | Trace visibly fails to fit, then remains available |
| A rough measurement disproves an exact relationship | Measure thick rail art in Quest 2 | Ideal centre lines reveal drawing error versus geometric reasoning |
| Parallel means “looks horizontal” | Rotate parallel rails together | Relation and notation persist |
| Corresponding means “same colour” | Fade colour cues in Quest 4 | Location remains; decorative colour disappears |
| Every zigzag pair is alternate | Offer an inside/outside mismatch in Quest 5 | Non-matching locations stay highlighted for comparison |
| Same-side interior angles are equal | Attempt to overlay them in Quest 6 | They differ but join into 180° |
| Lines that look parallel must be parallel | Illusion tunnel in Quest 7 | Angle verification overrides appearance |
| A theorem works only in the original orientation | Rotate and mirror later crossings | Relationship remains attached to structure |

## 10. Dialogue and performance rules

### Character balance

- **Nova** wonders, predicts, reacts, and names the discovered pattern.
- **The child avatar** notices, chooses, acts, and explains part of the result.
- **Rider** creates friendly story momentum, performs demonstrations, and
  occasionally makes a plausible mistaken assumption.

Nova cannot speak more than two consecutive turns without another character
acting or responding.

### Dialogue limits

- Most lines contain 4–10 words.
- One idea per bubble.
- Maximum two short lines in a bubble.
- Bubbles sit near speakers and never cover the active rail or angle.
- Audio and captions start together; replay remains available.
- Emotional direction is written per line: playful, surprised, puzzled,
  relieved, or excited—not a generic “friendly” voice for every scene.

### No narrator language

Reject lines such as:

- “Today we will learn corresponding angles.”
- “Select the correct answer.”
- “Excellent! You earned ten points.”
- “As you can see from the diagram…”

Prefer reactions to the shared scene:

- “Wait—that corner moved with it.”
- “They look equal. Want to test it?”
- “Different sizes. One straight bar.”

## 11. Interaction and visual rules

- Use the recurring physical rhythm:
  `skate → encounter → park safely → manipulate the course → discover →
  skate through the repaired section`.
- Skating carries the adventure and transitions. It does not need to impersonate
  every geometrical relationship as a skateboard trick.
- The mathematical action occurs in the course, not in an overlay panel.
- Straight movement is genuinely straight.
- Wheels maintain board and ground contact.
- Nova steps aside and holds the skateboard whenever another rider crosses
  their path.
- Characters look toward the currently changing relationship.
- The selected child avatar remains visible during every main action.
- Angle traces use shape and position, not colour alone.
- Labels appear beside the exact object they name.
- Formal notation appears after discovery and remains available without
  dominating the screen.
- Controls use large touch targets and never require fine degree placement.
- Wrong-way exploration changes the world and provides evidence; it does not
  erase progress.

### Phone composition at 375 × 812

The default portrait frame reserves:

- top 12% for compact Home, map, and progress controls;
- central 58% for characters and the mathematical course;
- bottom 22% for the immediate physical control;
- remaining space for one speaker bubble that avoids the active object.

Only one interaction control group and one active relationship appear at a
time.

### Reduced motion

Movement may become:

- start and end poses;
- persistent route traces;
- expanding/contracting angle wedges;
- focus rings and matched outlines;
- sequential light changes.

The mathematical consequence must remain fully understandable.

## 12. Progress, replay, and pacing

- Quest state persists after Home, refresh, and reopen.
- Previous Scene restores the exact rail position, selected trace, and
  discovered labels.
- Journal replay remains interactive but never adds duplicate rewards.
- The child may rest after Quests 2 and 4 without losing narrative continuity.
- Later rest is available after Quest 6 through the normal Home/Journal flow.
- The full chapter does not demand one uninterrupted sitting.

The replay of an earlier quest returns to the Journal. A live return moves
forward with the child’s saved state.

## 13. Learning evidence

The chapter is successful when the child can do most of the following without
being led to the answer:

1. Extend straight traces and decide whether they intersect.
2. Find the opposite angle at a crossing and show why it matches.
3. Join a linear pair into 180°.
4. Distinguish parallel from merely separated lines.
5. Identify a transversal and a corresponding pair by position.
6. Find alternate and same-side interior relationships after rotation.
7. Verify or construct a parallel line using one converse.
8. Carry one angle value through a short relationship chain.
9. Explain at least one idea in everyday words, even without the formal term.

Speed, perfect vocabulary, and immediate success are not required.

### Child-test prompts

- “What changed when you turned the rail?”
- “Show me the corner that moves with this one.”
- “How do you know those rails stay apart?”
- “Which corner is in the same position?”
- “What fooled your eyes in the tunnel?”
- “How did the 65° light reach the final turn?”
- “What would you show another child?”

Record:

- where the child experiments voluntarily;
- where they hesitate;
- whether they can predict before acting;
- whether Nova’s explanation clarifies or merely repeats;
- whether they replay;
- whether they can transfer after the visual cues fade.
